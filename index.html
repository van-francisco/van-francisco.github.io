01 / Independent digital learning product

Scholar's Road

A browser app that records Japanese study across different resources.

Audience People studying Japanese on their own    My role Product design, learning progression, interface and story direction, testing

The problem

My Japanese study spans vocabulary apps, textbooks, listening, reading, and conversation lessons. I built one place to record that work and see which skills I had practiced.

The product

I designed a log for ten kinds of practice, skill progress, levels, visual rewards, and interactive stories. I directed the interface and tested successive builds.

I reviewed AI-assisted code and artwork against my design requirements.

Gamification / Scholar's Road

Logging study and earning levels

Learners log Japanese study they complete using other resources. The app assigns experience points (XP) to the skills practiced.

01Log practice

Record the activity, date, and time spent studying.

02Update progress

The app updates progress in the skills practiced.

03Level up

After earning enough XP, use the level-up screen to advance.

Rewards for leveling up

Leveling up unlocks new versions of the study room and illustrated items for the learner to collect.

Interactive stories

The app has ten interactive story episodes. The first is available immediately; others unlock as the learner levels up. Each episode has two choices, and saved choices can affect later story text.

Testing and review

I tested saving and restoring progress, smaller-screen layouts, and keyboard navigation. The app remains in development.

02 / Grade 5 classroom curriculum

Fictional Narrative Writer's Notebook

Grade 5 writing lessons, including three notebook versions with different levels of support.

Audience Fifth-grade students    Standards California Grade 5 English language arts

Original assignment

Benchmark Advance, a published Grade 5 English language arts program, asks students to write a fictional story about a family that sees a whale from a boat. They must use factual details from nonfiction whale readings.

What I developed

I developed lessons for reading a sample story, choosing whale facts, planning a plot, drafting, revising, and giving feedback to classmates. Students then write a new story about a tide-pool visit.

The notebook includes a writing rubric adapted from the Smarter Balanced assessment criteria.

Three versions of the writing notebook

All three versions start with the same whale story. The planning prompts and amount of writing support change.

Standard

Open-ended questions let students plan the events, dialogue, and descriptions themselves.

Scaffolded English

A five-part planner provides questions about events, choices, consequences, and the ending.

English / Japanese

Japanese directions and picture choices support planning before students write in English.

Revision in each notebook

Students choose a sentence to expand, combine, or shorten.

A checklist and example guide revisions to one scene.

A bilingual checklist guides one sentence revision.

Feedback and independent writing

A partner notes a strong detail and asks one question for the writer to address.

The writing rubric covers organization, detail, and grammar.

Students write a tide-pool story using new nonfiction readings.
